The signals

Six things a price chart cannot tell you.

Every number here comes from the chain — the launch transaction itself and everything that moved in the seconds after it.

01

Bundlers

Wallets that bought in the same block or slot as the launch. That is machine timing, not people reacting. Shown as both what they took and what they still hold — a bundler that already dumped is evidence of a bundled launch, not of a clean one.

02

Funding clusters

When one address paid for a dozen wallets that all bought at the same instant. The clearest bundling signal there is, and nothing else on the page will show it to you.

03

Dev wallet

What the creator holds now, and how much of its initial allocation has already left the wallet.

04

Snipers

Buyers inside the first seconds, counted apart from bundlers so one wallet is never held against a token twice.

05

Real concentration

The top ten wallets with liquidity pools, burn addresses and program accounts removed. Counting a pool as a holder makes every healthy token look rugged.

06

Token controls

Whether anyone can still mint new supply, freeze your balance, take tokens out of any wallet, or change the transfer tax.

When it can't tell

It says unknown instead of guessing.

Some tokens have traded so heavily that their launch is no longer readable. Most tools quietly return zero for whatever they could not check — and a row of zeroes looks exactly like a clean token.

SONAR refuses to. When it cannot measure enough to have an opinion it withholds the score entirely, because a green number nobody earned is worse than no number at all.

Permissions

It runs where your wallet is. So it does almost nothing.

The entire permission list is three lines and you can read all of it. No tabs, no cookies, no webRequest, no access to any site but Fomo.

  • ✕ Never touches your wallet or signs anything
  • ✕ Never reads cookies, storage or session data
  • ✕ Never clicks, types or submits on the page
  • ✕ No analytics, no tracking, no remote code
"permissions": ["storage"], "content_scripts": [{ "matches": ["https://fomo.family/*"] }]

The whole of it. storage remembers where you dragged the panel; nothing else is kept, and nothing leaves your browser except the token's address.
